Keep approval at the release gate

To the leadership team · Arnold OS self-review · Evidence as of 19 July 2026

Decision requested

Keep overnight diagnosis and preparation of changes under supervision. Retain human approval before release. Before expanding autonomy, assign a second reviewer and agree which exceptions require escalation. Management can defer further investment while it measures the cost and benefit of this change.

Where the operation stood

In the July review, the orchestrator could take an alert through diagnosis and prepare a pull request for review. Every release and escalation depended on one operator. The review placed the system at rung 4. Its evidence for rung 5 was partial.

Capability rungEvidence in the review
0 · Data and BI consumptionOperators used inventory, failure and freshness reports.
1 · Assisted authoringOperators worked interactively with AI assistants.
2 · Grounded self-service Q&AThe inbox answered status questions from live queries.
3 · Supervised workflow automationMulti-step runs produced work for human review.
4 · Supervised autonomous agentsA bounded overnight task could reach a review checkpoint.
5 · Governed multi-agent workflowsPartial. Agents coordinated, but one operator still reviewed every irreversible output.

What management needs before expanding

Ownership and data. Name a backup reviewer. For a bounded trial, record the decision owner, outcome, elapsed approval time and rework for each proposed change. Compare both reviewers’ escalation decisions before delegating release authority.

Economics. The July review attributed USD 74.73 per month of compute usage to the autonomous tier within a USD 200 monthly pool. That figure alone cannot establish savings per approved change. Measure operator time and accepted outcomes alongside compute and hosting costs.

Model choice. The evidence describes routing smaller models to routine checks. It contains no open-weight comparison. Before changing models or moving to metered billing, replay representative tasks and compare acceptance, escalation and cost per accepted outcome.
